# Step 4.2 — Evidence review (documentation + measurement literature)

Access date for every web source below: **2026-11-11**. No dataset was downloaded; no waveform, header of a non-training record, model, or prior artifact was touched. Pages were read through a fetch tool; where only an automatic summary (not verbatim text) was available it is marked **[summary]**; sources that could not be read are marked **BLOCKED** and **no claim is taken from them**. Legend for status: **[V]** verified from readable primary text · **[H]** my inference · **[U]** unresolved. Nothing here is a clinical cutoff of mine; every numeric criterion is attributed to a source or labelled as an engineering convention.

## 3. Evidence per candidate measurement

**3.1 Ventricular rate / RR.** [V] R-peak detectors are mature on clean data (S13: F1 > 99% on high-quality single-lead data, > 95% on arrhythmia/NSR sets for nine of ten detectors) but degrade on poor-quality data (S13: all < 80%; S14: ranking changes under sample-accurate scoring and motion noise). [V] No source validated any detector on 12-lead, 500 Hz, 10 s resting hospital ECGs with arrhythmias; S13's arrhythmia robustness may be optimistic because some detectors were tuned on MIT-BIH (S13 notes this; my reading). [H] Multi-lead evidence is mentioned only as a suggested remedy in S13 ("multilead fusion … could be a promising method"). Evidence supports the *concept* of an R-peak-derived rate; it does not validate any specific implementation on our data (extrapolation across sampling rate, leads, population).

**3.2 Short-record RR irregularity.** [V] S25 validates RMSSD/SDNN from 10 s segments **against 240–300 s references in a sinus-rhythm-edited, finger-pressure data set**, with inflated agreement statistics acknowledged and SDNN judged a poor proxy; S26 calls ultra-short measures controversial and recommends Bland–Altman agreement; S33 uses 60-RR windows for AF-burden detection. [V] The standard (S27) could not be read. [H] Therefore a 10 s RR-irregularity statistic computed on **all detected intervals (RR, not NN) in arrhythmia-containing records** is a *descriptive window statistic*, not validated conventional HRV, and the sinus-rhythm validity evidence does not transfer to AF, flutter or ectopy. It may still be usable as a clearly-defined concept, validated against reference R-peaks, with that wording.

**3.3 Signal quality.** [V] SQI definitions exist (S21) but were validated on single-lead ICU data and the authors warn that SQIs can label arrhythmia as noise; CinC 2011 (S22) shows expert acceptability grading on 12-lead 500 Hz data is feasible but grade-based and about suitability for interpretation, not for each measurement. The origin and definition of detector-agreement SQI (bSQI) could not be verified (S23/S24 blocked). [H] Quality should therefore be layered and measurement-specific, with thresholds treated as engineering parameters set on training data.

**3.4 Frontal QRS axis (conditional).** [V] The accessible open sources contain **no validated automated axis algorithm**: S31 is a small physiological study without a reference, S32 uses an unnamed device report, S10/S06 give only categorical axis statements (PTB-XL from reports; LUDB categories). The AHA/ACCF/HRS statements that define the standard were not accessible (S28). [H] Without delineation, a fixed R-centred window mixes QRS with ST/T for wide QRS and clips wide complexes; lead scaling, baseline definition and the 4.88 µV vs 1 µV calibration question are unresolved. **Not adequately supported → deferred** (design notes only in `measurement_protocol_draft.md`).

**3.5 QRS amplitude and low voltage (conditional).** [V] Published low-voltage definitions in accessible sources are clinical papers' own, mutually inconsistent in units and lead groups (S29, S30); the official criteria were not accessible. [H] A per-lead amplitude is a measurement; "low voltage" is a classification requiring an authoritative definition and manual-validated amplitudes. Calibration is unresolved and filtering history unknown (raw vs denoised). **Deferred.**

## 4. R-peak detector comparison (candidates, not a decision)

| Candidate | What the evidence says | Fit to our data | Availability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pan–Tompkins family (S18 citation; S13 variants; S14) | S13: > 95% on arrhythmia/NSR single-lead sets, runtime a few ms; S14 [summary]: weaker than EngZee/Elgendi under sample-accurate JF on noisy exercise data; original paper unreadable | Designed for one lead at a fixed rate; adaptive thresholds on 10 s need burn-in handling | implementable with scipy; also in NeuroKit2/wfdb (not installed) |
| Hamilton (S20 citation; S13 'Hamilton-mean/median') | S13: comparable to Pan–Tompkins; original paper not read | Same as above | implementable; NeuroKit2 `hamilton2002` |
| Elgendi two moving averages (S15) | Two parameters (97 ms, 611 ms) tuned on MIT-BIH, tested on 10 databases (lead I only); published parameters at 360 Hz must be re-expressed in ms for 500 Hz; offset unresolved | Simple, scipy-implementable; may be sensitive to ectopic amplitude changes (unverified) | open article; NeuroKit2 `elgendi2010/2013` |
| EngZee Mod / WQRS (S14) | S14 [summary]: best under noise on GUDB | Domain (250 Hz, motion) differs from resting 500 Hz | NeuroKit2 `engzeemod2012`; WQRS in WFDB (not documented as `xqrs`) |
| Kalidas–Tamil SWT (S19) | Only abstract-level claims seen in search text | unknown | NeuroKit2 `kalidas2017` |
| WFDB `xqrs` (S17) | Documented; band-pass 5–20 Hz | not evaluated here | requires wfdb package |

[H] Selection should be by (i) availability of a documented algorithm that can be run without new dependencies, (ii) multi-lead fusion on 12 leads, (iii) disagreement between at least two *different-principle* detectors as an uncertainty indicator, and (iv) validation against expert R-peaks — **not** by popularity. No detector can be declared best from the evidence (domains differ; two key papers unreadable). The proposed comparison is in `method_decision_table.csv` and `step4_3_pilot_plan.md`.
